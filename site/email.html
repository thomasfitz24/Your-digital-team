●03 · How it actually works

A great subject line in the spam folder.

This is the part most people never look at. An email that doesn't reach the inbox has a 0% open rate no matter how well it was written — and the mailbox providers decide that before anyone sees a word of it.

Three DNS records do most of the work: SPF says which servers may send as you, DKIM signs the message so it can't be tampered with, and DMARC tells providers what to do when something fails. Get those wrong and you are anonymous mail from an unverified sender.

The rest is reputation, and reputation is mostly about restraint: emailing people who actually want to hear from you, removing the ones who don't, and never buying a list. One bad send can cost you months of inbox placement.

The order we do it in.

Sending to a list in poor health is how a domain gets a reputation it takes months to repair. Hygiene first.

01

Authentication records, sender reputation, hard bounces and the dormant contacts quietly damaging your placement. We fix all of it before a single campaign goes out — because sending to a list in poor health is how a domain earns a reputation that takes months to repair.

02

Welcome, abandoned basket, post-purchase, win-back. These are a fraction of the sends and about half the revenue, and once they're live they keep working with no ongoing effort. Newsletters are the layer on top — they should never be the whole strategy.

03

One message to everybody is the fastest route to being ignored, and then to being marked as spam. We split by behaviour, lifecycle stage and value, so a repeat customer and someone who signed up yesterday don't get the same email. Smaller sends to the right people beat bigger sends every time.

04

Templates that hold up in Outlook and in dark mode, subject lines written to be opened by the right person rather than the most people, and one clear action per email. If a message is asking for three things it's really asking for none.

05

Open rates have been unreliable since Apple started pre-loading images, and they were always the wrong target anyway — a curiosity-gap subject line wins the open and loses the sale. We settle every test on revenue, report it weekly in plain English, and retire the losers without arguing for them.
