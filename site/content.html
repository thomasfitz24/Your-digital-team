●03 · How it actually works

Your best page is dying quietly.

Nobody warns you about this one. A page peaks somewhere around month eight, and then starts losing perhaps 3% of its traffic a month — slowly enough that nobody notices, because everyone is looking at the total rather than at individual pages.

It isn't that the writing got worse. Competitors published something newer, the facts aged, and the search itself shifted. Left alone for two years a strong page quietly halves.

A refresh — updated figures, the questions people now ask, a restructure to match what currently ranks — costs a fraction of a new piece and usually recovers more traffic than a new piece would have won. Which is why we budget for maintaining the library, not just for adding to it.

●How we work

The order we do it in.

Publishing more is the easiest thing to sell and the least likely to work. Everything here happens before volume.

01

Most sites we take on are already sitting on pages that could rank and don't. We inventory everything, measure what each piece actually earns, and separate the ones worth refreshing from the ones worth merging or deleting. Thin pages competing with each other for the same query are a common and completely fixable problem — and fixing it costs nothing to write.

02

A page can rank first for a term with four thousand searches a month and produce almost nothing, while a page ranking third for six hundred fills your diary. We prioritise by what the searcher is trying to do, and we'll show you the working — including when the honest answer is that a topic isn't worth writing about.

03

The query, the intent behind it, the questions that must be answered and in what order, a length taken from what currently ranks rather than invented, and the internal links it has to carry. Nearly every piece of content that fails, fails here — by the time a draft is wrong, the decisions that made it wrong were taken days earlier.

04

Answer in the first eighty words. Structure so it can be skimmed and still make sense. One clear action. No throat-clearing introduction about how important the topic is. Search engines have spent years getting better at spotting content written for search engines, and the defence against that has always been the same: write for the person.

05

Published is not finished. Pages decay from about month eight, and a refresh usually recovers more traffic than a new piece would have won for less than the cost. Part of every month goes on updating what's already live — which is the part almost nobody budgets for and the reason most content libraries quietly shrink.
