Shoreline 911 against Singer, Gunther Werks and the field

Shoreline's site already looks like it belongs in this category. What it lacks is the conversion machinery every serious competitor has built around the photography: specs on the cards, a visible commissioning process, proof, and a contact path that cannot fail.

How this was done

I loaded each site in Chromium at desktop and phone widths, captured full-page screenshots, and pulled rendered metrics from the live DOM: type sizes, fonts, calls to action, tap-target sizes, image counts and alt text, forms, page weight. I then read every page's content and checked it against the skill's UX checklist (accessibility, touch, performance, style, layout, typography, forms, navigation).

Sites reviewed: Shoreline 911 (home, builds, GTR Modern, about, contact, gallery, blog), Singer Vehicle Design, Gunther Werks, Theon Design, Prinzip R, Paul Stephens, Everrati, Kalmar Automotive, Workshop 5001, Tuthill Porsche.

Limits: Tuthill, Kalmar and Emory block automated browsers, so they were reviewed from their page content and press coverage only. Singer, Gunther Werks and Theon blocked image loading from my environment, so their visual notes come from content and structure rather than screenshots. Everything about Shoreline is first-hand.

What the competitors do that Shoreline does not

Singer

singervehicledesign.com · Los Angeles
  • Opens with a manifesto, not a product: "A relentless pursuit of excellence," then three tenets. The car is assumed.
  • One header action, "Connect," and two links under every feature: "Inquire Now | Learn More." Nothing else competes.
  • Navigation sells a world: Ownership, Willow Springs, Cinema, Timepieces, Partners. Ownership is a page, not a paragraph.
  • Condensed display type at 72 px against 16 px body. Extreme contrast in scale is the whole visual system.

Gunther Werks

guntherwerks.com · Huntington Beach
  • Every program tile carries the number that matters: "850 hp twin-turbo. Limited to 75." "1,067 hp. Limited to 26."
  • A six-stage build section (Customization, Donor disassembly, Fabrication, Body, Paint, Assembly), each with its own page.
  • Ownership block: GW Access, Track Experience, Events, Provenance Succession, plus a 5-year / 100,000-mile warranty headline.
  • Accessibility toolbar (font size, contrast, reduced motion), language and region toggles, nine videos on the home page.

Theon Design

theondesign.com · Oxfordshire
  • The first thing on the home page is "What the media say," a run of journalist quotes.
  • Model pages end in a real technical specification: 3.6 to 4.0 L, 420+ bhp, about 1,150 kg, Hewland gearbox, KW or TracTive suspension.
  • Phone number sits in the header on every page.

Prinzip R

prinzipr.com · Germany · closest direct backdate rival
  • "Request your individual consultation" appears in the header, after the tiers, after the process and in the footer. On phones it is a sticky bar.
  • Three tier cards, each with a one-line spec: "ST-widebody in steel, 964 donor, up to 400 hp."
  • "Known from" press logos, a five-star client quote, seven recent builds, a five-step process and an FAQ that answers duration (12 to 15 months) and entry price (from 200k EUR net).
  • Lead magnet: "Get the PRINZIP R Dossier" email capture.

Everrati

everrati.com · UK
  • Proper inquiry form: name, email, phone, country, a "what can we help with" dropdown, message, consent.
  • Configurator link in the navigation, twelve media logos, upcoming events list.

Paul Stephens and Kalmar

Dealer-style patterns
  • Paul Stephens: "AVAILABLE" badge on every listing, phone and WhatsApp side by side, "free valuation" as a second CTA.
  • Kalmar: one platform, eight named variants (Classic, Evergreen, Cabriolet, Air, Evo, E-Volt, Turbo), a Selected Dealers page and a newsletter.

Two things the whole field avoids: nobody but Prinzip R publishes a price on the site, and nobody offers self-serve configuration except Everrati. Shoreline already states "From $375,000" on the GTR Modern page and a $350k to $400k range in the blog. That honesty is a differentiator, and right now it is hidden.

Shoreline findings, in priority order

  1. The inquiry form is an embed with no fallback Critical

    The contact page has no native form. It loads a HubSpot form via an external script. When that script is slow or blocked, the page shows a headline and a black void. Two of my three loads rendered exactly that. No phone number appears on the contact page, the about page or the builds page. The address is "Miami, FL" with no street.

    Fix: add a native Squarespace form (name, email, phone, country, which build, message) and keep HubSpot as the destination via the integration rather than an embed. Put the phone number and a WhatsApp link in the header on every page, as Theon and Paul Stephens do. Add the street address or "by appointment, Miami" with a map.

  2. Build cards carry no information Critical

    Each tile shows a name, an "Explore" link and, on the builds page, a "Build Slots Available Now" chip. There is no engine, power, donor, price or lead time. The GTR Modern page has all of it: 3.8 L, six-speed, from $375,000, slots from Q4 2026. Gunther Werks puts the two most persuasive numbers on every tile.

    Fix: a card spec is below. The missing images are a separate defect: the Modern and Evo tiles are served with format=original (full-size uploads) while the others use sized variants. Re-upload or re-crop those two so Squarespace serves responsive sizes.

  3. The hero has two competing actions and neither is the strong one Critical

    "View 911 Backdate Inventory" is styled as plain text under the headline, easy to miss against the photograph. "Build Slots Available Now" is the only real button, parked bottom-right and pointing at the same page. On phones the button's label changes to "Available Now," which no longer reads as an action.

    Fix: one primary button, "See the builds," placed under the subhead. One secondary text link, "Start a commission." Remove the section counters ("01/Home", "02/About"); they are template chrome with no meaning for a buyer.

  4. No proof anywhere on the site High

    There are no press mentions, testimonials, delivery count, awards or event appearances on any page. Theon leads with journalist quotes, Prinzip R with press logos and a client quote, Everrati with twelve media logos. Shoreline's own blog states what has been delivered, but the home page does not.

    Fix: a proof strip under the hero: builds delivered, countries shipped to, years of the parent group, and any press. If press is thin, use two client quotes with name and city, and a photo of a delivered car with its owner.

  5. The commissioning process is never explained High

    Nothing on the site says what happens after "get in touch": consultation, donor sourcing, deposit, specification, build, delivery, or how long it takes. Gunther Werks shows six stages, Prinzip R five steps with a 12 to 15 month answer in the FAQ. Shoreline has already written this content in the blog post "The build process," so it costs copy, not research.

    Fix: a "How a Shoreline is built" section on the home and about pages with five steps and a stated build time, plus a short FAQ (price range, donor, deposit, timeline, shipping, warranty).

  6. The best content is unreachable from the navigation High

    The sitemap lists 27 blog posts (pricing, 964 vs 993, cost breakdown, manifesto) and a gallery of about 45 images. Neither Blog nor Gallery appears in the header or footer. Meanwhile "Defenders" sends visitors to a different site from the main menu.

    Fix: navigation of Builds, Process, Gallery, Journal, About, Contact. Move Defenders to the footer as "Also from Shoreline: Defenders."

  7. Pricing exists but is buried High

    "From $375,000" is on the GTR Modern page and the full range is in a blog post. The cards and builds page say nothing. In a category where nobody publishes prices, a visible "from" price filters tyre-kickers and signals confidence.

    Fix: "From $350,000" on each card and a one-line "What's included" under the builds heading. Keep the blog post as the long answer.

  8. Typography is cramped and inconsistent Medium

    Body copy is Helvetica Neue with letter-spacing forced to -0.05em, which squeezes running text. Three families are in play: Helvetica Neue for headings and body, Poppins for the nav, Manrope for the builds-page headline. Mobile body text is 14 px on the home page and 13 px on the builds page; the guideline minimum is 16 px.

    Fix: one family for body at 17 to 18 px desktop, 16 px mobile, letter-spacing at 0. Keep the tight tracking for headlines only. Pick either Poppins or Manrope for labels and drop the other.

  9. Section labels look like buttons Medium

    "ABOUT," "OUR BUILDS" and "CONTACT US" are drawn as outlined pills identical to the real buttons ("Read More," "Explore") but are not clickable. Eleven of thirteen tappable elements on the phone home page are under 44 px tall.

    Fix: render section labels as plain uppercase eyebrows with letter-spacing, no border. Raise button height to 44 px minimum on touch.

  10. Pages are heavy for what they show Medium

    The builds page transfers about 5 MB, 3.9 MB of it images, to show four cars. The home page is 2.5 MB with nine images. Prinzip R delivers a far longer page in 1 MB using AVIF and WebP. Two images on the site have no alt text, and there is no video anywhere.

    Fix: re-export hero and card imagery at 2500 px max, let Squarespace serve WebP, and add a 20-second driving clip to the hero. Sound and motion are the product for an air-cooled car.

  11. The about page under-sells the workshop Medium

    Team bios cover technology, hospitality and real estate. Nobody on the page builds cars, and there are no workshop photos, no engineering partners named, and one broken image slot ("911 badge detail"). Singer, Gunther Werks and Theon all show hands, metal and the facility.

    Fix: name the build partner or head technician, show the shop, list the engineering suppliers already mentioned on the GTR page (TracTive, Fuchs, Michelin, PCCM), and fix the missing image.

Recommended home page order

  • Hero: headline, one sentence, one button, short driving clip with sound off.
  • Proof strip: builds delivered, countries, press or client quotes.
  • The range: four cards as specified above, "From $350,000" visible.
  • How it is built: five steps, stated build time, deposit note.
  • Craft detail: three close-ups (engine, interior, paint) with a line each. The GTR page copy already exists.
  • Ownership: warranty, servicing, shipping, events. Even two sentences beats silence.
  • Journal: three latest posts, since the writing is already strong.
  • Contact: native form, phone, WhatsApp, address, hours.
